Propellant Sloshing — theory

Liquid motion in a partially filled cylindrical tank. Based on C. P. Hoult, An Analysis of the Motion of Liquids in a Partially-Filled Enclosure, with its 2013 errata.

Open the calculatorOriginal report (PDF)

Overview

The report gives force and moment transfer functions of sloshing propellant for ballistic-missile autopilot analysis. It was the first such analysis for circular tanks. Its key assumptions are:

  • the liquid is inviscid and incompressible, and its motion is small, so the free-surface condition can be linearised;
  • the tank is a rigid upright circular cylinder;
  • the axial acceleration a=(T-D)/m is steady over the interval of interest.

The natural frequencies are proportional to \sqrt a, so a varying thrust acceleration would make the problem non-linear. In practice the variation over the time of interest is small, and the frequencies are evaluated at the current acceleration.

Natural frequencies

The velocity potential is a sum of terms in J_1(\lambda_n r/R)\cos\varphi, multiplied by hyperbolic functions of depth. No flow through the wall requires

J_1^\prime(\lambda_n)=0:\qquad \lambda_1=1.84118,\ \lambda_2=5.33144,\ \lambda_3=8.53632,\ \dots

These are the modern values given in the errata. The linearised free-surface condition then gives the natural frequencies, for a fill depth h=M/(\rho\pi R^2):

\omega_n^2=\frac{a}{R}\,\lambda_n\tanh\frac{\lambda_n h}{R}

The report writes the argument as \lambda_nM/(\pi\rho R^3), which is the same thing.

Two limits are useful:

  • For a deep fill, \tanh\to1 and \omega_n^2\to a\lambda_n/R, independent of depth.
  • For a shallow fill, \omega_n\to\lambda_n\sqrt{ah}/R, the shallow-water wave speed.

Equivalent mechanical model

For vehicle dynamics each mode can be replaced by a pendulum (or a spring–mass) that produces the same side force at the same frequency. Following Abramson (NASA SP-106), mode n has slosh mass and pendulum length

\frac{m_n}{M}=\frac{2\tanh(\lambda_nh/R)}{\lambda_n(\lambda_n^2-1)\,h/R},\qquad L_n=\frac{R}{\lambda_n\tanh(\lambda_nh/R)}

so that \omega_n^2=a/L_n. The rest of the liquid moves with the tank.

Because \sum_n 2/(\lambda_n^2-1)=1, almost all of a shallow fill sloshes, about 84 % of it in the first mode. In a deep tank only a small top layer does. The higher modes carry little mass, which is why the first mode dominates stability analyses.

Verification

tests/slosh.test.js checks the following:

  • the ten Bessel roots against J_1^\prime=0, evaluated from Bessel’s integral;
  • the frequency formula, including its deep and shallow limits;
  • the pendulum analogue;
  • the slosh-mass sum rule.

Notes on the method

  • Fill depth. The frequencies use \tanh(\lambda_nh/R) with h=M/(\rho\pi R^2), as in the report. The tabulated example puts the density where the propellant mass belongs, which removes the effect of fill depth.
  • Equivalent mechanical model. The slosh masses and pendulum lengths are the standard model of NASA SP-106. The report expresses the same physics as transfer functions.
  • Not modelled. Baffles, tank-bottom shape and damping. Baffles mainly add damping; they change the frequencies only by changing the tank geometry.
