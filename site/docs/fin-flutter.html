Fin Flutter Margin — theory

An approximate bending-torsion flutter check for fin panels. The workbook FLUTTER MARGIN1.xls implements the correlation of D. J. Martin, Summary of Flutter Experiences as a Guide to the Preliminary Design of Lifting Surfaces on Missiles, NACA TN-4197, 1958.

Overview

Aeroelasticity covers the static twisting of fins under load and a dynamic phenomenon, flutter, in which a fin’s bending and torsion modes couple with the airflow and extract energy from it. Once flutter starts the amplitude grows within a few cycles and the fin usually fails. After the Prospector P-8 fin-flutter failure, the rsandt.com notes say, “everyone should be sensitive to the possibility of flutter”.

The model here is a rough approximation, intended to be good enough to keep the designer out of trouble. It reduces flight and wind-tunnel experience to one correlation for the flutter Mach number of a fin panel. The output is a margin of safety against the flight Mach number.

Panel geometry

For one exposed trapezoidal panel with root chord c_R, tip chord c_T, exposed semispan b and uniform thickness t:

\lambda=\frac{c_T}{c_R},\qquad S=\frac{(c_R+c_T)\,b}{2},\qquad A\!R=\frac{b^2}{S}.

The thickness ratio is taken at the chord three-quarters of the way out along the span, which for a straight-tapered panel is

c_{0.75}=\tfrac14c_R+\tfrac34c_T,\qquad \frac{t}{c}=\frac{t}{c_{0.75}}.

Sweep does not appear in the correlation.

The flutter correlation

The geometry enters through one parameter,

X=\frac{39.3\,A\!R^3}{(t/c)^3\,(A\!R+2)},

and the flutter Mach number is

M_f=\sqrt{\frac{G}{X\,\dfrac{\lambda+1}{2}\,\dfrac{p}{p_0}}},

with the material shear modulus G in psi and p/p_0 the ratio of static pressure at the flight altitude to sea-level pressure. The flutter speed is M_f times the local speed of sound.

The trends are worth remembering: M_f\propto\sqrt{G} (stiffer material helps), M_f\propto(t/c)^{3/2} (thickness helps most), and M_f\propto(p/p_0)^{-1/2} (low altitude is the critical place).

Pressure ratio

The workbook evaluates the pressure ratio with a single-layer tropospheric law:

\frac{p}{p_0}=\left(1-\frac{0.0035662\,h}{T_{SL}}\right)^{32.174/(1716\times0.0035662)}=\left(1-\frac{0.0035662\,h}{T_{SL}}\right)^{5.2575},

with altitude h in feet above sea level and T_{SL}=551.8 °R (about 92 °F, a hot day). The calculator exposes T_{SL} as an input so a standard day (518.67 °R) can be used. The law holds in the troposphere. Above the tropopause (36 089 ft) the calculator continues it as an isothermal layer, p/p_0=(p/p_0)_T\,e^{-g(h-h_T)/(RT_T)}, matching the standard atmosphere; the single-layer law alone would turn negative near 120 000–155 000 ft.

Margin of safety and minimum thickness

The margin of safety compares the flutter Mach number with the flight Mach number M:

MS=\frac{M_f-M}{M}.

A negative margin means flutter is predicted. Check the worst point on the trajectory: usually maximum velocity at low altitude.

Setting M_f=M and solving for the thickness gives the thickness at which the margin is zero — the calculator’s thickness for zero margin:

X_{crit}=\frac{G}{M^2\,\frac{\lambda+1}{2}\,\frac{p}{p_0}},\qquad t_{min}=c_{0.75}\left[\frac{39.3\,A\!R^3}{X_{crit}\,(A\!R+2)}\right]^{1/3}.

This inversion is an addition by this site, using the same correlation. Choose a design thickness comfortably above t_{min}.

Assumptions and limits

  • An empirical correlation for thin, flat, cantilevered panels of uniform thickness and isotropic material. For composites, G should be the laminate’s in-plane shear modulus, which can be far below its tensile modulus.
  • Root fixity is assumed. Flexible fin attachments lower the true flutter speed.
  • Static aeroelastic twist (loss of roll control or divergence) is a separate effect, covered by the related memo “Effects of Fin Static Aeroelasticity on Roll Rate” and the TWISTIT workbook (not yet a calculator here).
  • The calculator highlights margins below 0.5 as “thin”. That threshold is this site’s guidance, not part of the source.

Worked example (Gold Rush III)

Aluminium 6061 fins, G=3.8\times10^6 psi, c_R=10 in, c_T=2 in, b=5.5 in, t=0.125 in, at M=0.915 and 7,686 ft:

  1. \lambda=0.2, S=33 in², A\!R=0.9167, c_{0.75}=4 in, t/c=0.03125.
  2. X=39.3(0.9167)^3/\big((0.03125)^3(2.9167)\big)=340{,}087.
  3. p/p_0=0.76501.
  4. M_f=\sqrt{3.8\times10^6/(340087\times0.6\times0.76501)}=4.934.
  5. MS=(4.934-0.915)/0.915=4.39; zero margin would need only t_{min}=0.041 in.

Known differences from the workbook

  • Flight speed input. The workbook has a flight-speed cell (F6, 1037 ft/s) that no formula uses; the Mach number is entered separately (P6). The calculator asks only for the Mach number.
  • Sea-level temperature exposed. The workbook hard-codes 551.8 °R in the pressure ratio; the calculator makes it an input with that default. Its lapse rate (0.0035662 °R/ft) is the 1976 standard value, slightly different from the Geophysics memo’s 0.00357448.
  • Additions. Thickness for zero margin and the two plots are derived from the same correlation.
