Ogive Nose Profile — theory

Geometry of the tangent-ogive nose: the defining circle, the profile, and exact closed forms for wetted area and its centroid. Based on C. P. Hoult, Ogive Nose, RS&T memo, 6 May 2011, and the workbook Ogive Profile.xls.

Open the calculatorOriginal memo (PDF)

Overview

The ogive is the most common nose shape on sounding rockets. Its side profile is a single circular arc that is tangent to the body tube at the base — so there is no kink where nose meets tube — and that closes to a sharp point at the tip. Because the arc is fixed by only two numbers, the base radius R and the length L, every geometric property can be written in closed form.

The calculator returns the profile (for templates and CAD), the wetted area (for skin-friction drag and shell weight) and the area centroid (for centre-of-gravity estimates).

Geometry and the defining circle

Place the origin at the centre of the defining circle, with x along the rocket axis measured forward from the base plane and y perpendicular. The circle of radius a is

x^2+y^2=a^2 .

The centre lies in the base plane, a distance a-R below the axis, so the arc is horizontal (tangent to the tube) at x=0. The tip lies on the axis, y=a-R, at x=L. Substituting gives the defining radius:

L^2+(a-R)^2=a^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=\frac{L^2+R^2}{2R}.

Two further quantities follow directly. The fineness ratio is L/2R. The surface makes an angle with the axis at the tip of

\theta_{tip}=\sin^{-1}\!\frac{L}{a},

since the tangent at the tip is perpendicular to the radius drawn to it. (The tip angle is derived geometry added by this site; the workbook does not output it.)

Profile

The local body radius is the height of the arc above the axis:

r(x)=R-a+\sqrt{a^2-x^2},\qquad 0\le x\le L,

and the corresponding body station measured aft of the tip is L-x. The calculator evaluates the profile every \Delta x starting at the base (as the workbook does), and always includes the tip point. The increment affects only the table; area and centroid are exact integrals.

Wetted area

A strip of surface of axial width dx has area dS=2\pi r\sqrt{1+(dr/dx)^2}\,dx. With dr/dx=-x/\sqrt{a^2-x^2} the square root becomes a/\sqrt{a^2-x^2}, so

dS=2\pi a\left(1+\frac{R-a}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}\right)dx .

Integrating from the base to the tip,

S=2\pi a\left[(R-a)\sin^{-1}\frac{L}{a}+L\right].

Integrating instead from x=L-x_s to L gives the area between the tip and body station x_s,

S(x_s)=2\pi a\left[x_s+(R-a)\left(\sin^{-1}\frac{L}{a}-\sin^{-1}\frac{L-x_s}{a}\right)\right],

which is plotted on the calculator page. This cumulative curve is what the drag calculation needs to find the laminar wetted area ahead of boundary-layer transition.

For the memo’s example, R=3 in and L=30 in, the surface area is 379.2477 in².

Area centroid

The first moment of the strip about the base is dM=x\,dS:

dM=2\pi a\,x\left(1+\frac{R-a}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}\right)dx,
M=2\pi a\left[\tfrac12L^2+(R-a)\left(a-\sqrt{a^2-L^2}\right)\right].

The centroid lies M/S forward of the base, so its body station aft of the tip is

\bar x=L-\frac{M}{S}.

Memo example: \bar x=18.70511 in aft of the nose tip, 62.4 % of the nose length.

Shell mass

The memo notes that wetted area multiplied by wall thickness and material density gives the ogive weight. For a thin shell of uniform thickness t\ll R and density \rho,

m\approx S\,t\,\rho ,

with its centre of mass at the area centroid \bar x. Shoulders, bulkheads, tip inserts and ballast must be added separately. The approximation over-estimates slightly near the tip, where the inner surface is smaller than the outer.

Assumptions and limits

  • Tangent ogive only (the arc meets the tube with zero slope). Secant ogives and ogives with a blunted (spherical) tip are not covered. For a hemispherical tip see the Barrowman calculator, which caps the profile automatically.
  • Any consistent length unit works; the equations are scale-free.
  • a\ge L always holds because L^2+R^2\ge 2RL, so \sin^{-1}(L/a) is always defined.

Worked example

Take R=3 in, L=30 in (a 6-inch, 5-calibre nose):

  1. a=(30^2+3^2)/(2\cdot3)=151.5 in.
  2. S=2\pi(151.5)\left[(3-151.5)\sin^{-1}(30/151.5)+30\right]=379.248 in².
  3. M=2\pi(151.5)\left[450+(-148.5)(151.5-\sqrt{151.5^2-30^2})\right]=4283.56 in³.
  4. \bar x=30-4283.56/379.248=18.705 in aft of the tip.

Known differences from the workbook

  • Centroid label. The workbook heads cell L5 “Volume Centroid, in from Base”, but the formula =C5-K5/J5 gives the area centroid measured from the tip, as the memo states. The calculator labels it accordingly; the number is identical.
  • Additions. The tip half-angle, fineness ratio, cumulative-area curve and shell mass are not in the workbook. They are derived directly from the memo’s equations.
