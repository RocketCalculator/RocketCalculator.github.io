Body Bending Modes — theory

Natural bending frequencies of a non-uniform rocket. Based on C. P. Hoult, Longitudinal Body Bending Dynamics, 2013.

Open the calculatorOriginal memo (PDF)

Overview

Whenever flexible-body effects matter — propellant slosh, axial vibration, body bending — Lagrange’s equations come into play. A rocket in flight bends as a free-free beam: nothing holds either end. Its bending frequencies must stay clear of the pitch frequency, the roll rate and motor pressure oscillations, and sensors placed near a node of the fundamental mode see little bending motion.

Uniform beam and Rayleigh–Ritz

For a uniform free-free beam of length L, stiffness EI and mass per length m, the modes are

\psi_j(\xi)=B_j\left(\sinh\beta_j\xi+\sin\beta_j\xi\right)+\cosh\beta_j\xi+\cos\beta_j\xi,\qquad B_j=\frac{\cos\beta_j-\cosh\beta_j}{\sinh\beta_j-\sin\beta_j}

with \xi=x/L and \beta_j the roots of \cos\beta\cosh\beta=1: 4.7300, 7.8532, 10.9956. Their frequencies are \omega_j=(\beta_j/L)^2\sqrt{EI/m}.

A real rocket is a chain of sections — nose, payload, motor case, skirt — with their own stiffness and weight. Its displacement is approximated by the three uniform shapes, y=\sum_jq_j\psi_j(x/L). Lagrange’s equations with the kinetic energy \tfrac12\int m\dot y^2dx and strain energy \tfrac12\int EI\,(y'')^2dx give \mathbf M\ddot{\mathbf q}+\mathbf K\mathbf q=0, with

K_{ij}=\int_0^LEI\,\psi_i''\psi_j''\,dx,\qquad M_{ij}=\int_0^Lm\,\psi_i\psi_j\,dx

and the natural frequencies satisfy \det(\omega^2\mathbf M-\mathbf K)=0. The calculator integrates each section (stiffness and weight varying linearly within it) by Simpson’s rule and solves the 3×3 eigenproblem directly. The lowest frequency is the most accurate; with three shapes the third is only a rough estimate.

Mode shapes and nodes

Each eigenvector gives a mode shape \sum q_j\psi_j, shown scaled to 1 at the nose tip. The fundamental mode has two nodes where the body does not move; an inertial sensor there sees the least bending.

Verification

For the original field tool’s Aries example (five sections, 365 in), the tests reproduce its three frequencies — 257.6, 585.5 and 1523.7 rad/s — and its stiffness and mass integrals within 0.1–0.2 %. They also check that the mode shapes are exact eigenvectors, and that a uniform body returns the exact uniform-beam frequencies.

Notes on the method

  • Mode shapes of the field tool. Its mode-shape macro misspells one matrix element (l13st for j13st), so the term -K_{13} is dropped and its mode shapes are wrong, although its frequencies are right. The calculator uses the full eigenvectors; the test confirms that the typo reproduces the tool’s shapes exactly.
  • Integration. The tool’s trapezoid sums omit the first small step of each section; the calculator integrates the whole section, a difference of about 0.01 %.
  • Uniform reference. The uniform-beam frequencies shown for comparison use length-averaged stiffness and mass.
