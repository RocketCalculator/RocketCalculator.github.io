Flight Path Angle Dispersions — theory

How thrust misalignment and gusts rotate the velocity vector of a rolling rocket. Based on C. P. Hoult, Flight Path Angle Dispersions (rev. 1), 2011.

Open the calculatorOriginal memo (PDF)

Overview

Every perturbation in boost either changes the length of the velocity vector (an energy error) or rotates it. Sounding rockets fly nearly vertically, so apogee errors come mainly from energy errors and impact point errors mainly from rotations. Two perturbations rotate the vector: thrust misalignment, which rotates with the body, and wind gusts, which are fixed to the earth. Both act mostly at low altitude and low speed, just after the rail.

The analysis takes the phugoid (trajectory) part of the response, assumes the short-period oscillations damp promptly, and expresses the result as an equivalent launcher angle error: the launcher error that, with no perturbation, would give the same velocity tilt at burnout. It is conservative: the steady states it uses are upper bounds of the transient responses.

Thrust misalignment

A rocket rolling at roll wave number \lambda_R with a misalignment angle \delta_T and a thrust offset \Omega holds a steady angle of attack. Taking the two one-sigma effects as independent, its lift is constant along the boost:

L_A=qSC_{L\alpha}\alpha_{SS}=\frac{\rho SC_{L\alpha}T\sqrt{\ell^2\delta_T^2+\Omega^2}}{2I_p(\lambda_P^2-\lambda_R^2)}

It grows without bound as the roll wave number approaches the pitch wave number \lambda_P (roll resonance), which is why more static margin can cut the dispersion sharply. With speed U^2=2ah, acceleration a=T/m-g and the flight path angle \gamma_T measured from the vertical,

h\frac{d\gamma_T}{dh}=\frac{g}{2a}\gamma_T+\frac{L_A}{2ma},\quad \gamma_T(L)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad \gamma_T=\frac{L_A}{mg}\left[\left(\frac{h}{L}\right)^{g/2a}-1\right]

Most of the turn happens close to the launcher of length L; a longer rail reduces it.

Roll

The lift rotates with the rocket, at roll angle \phi=\lambda_R(h-L), so each increment of turn points in a different direction. Resolved into east and north components and summed in altitude steps,

E=\int\cos\phi\,d\gamma_T,\qquad N=\int\sin\phi\,d\gamma_T,\qquad \gamma_D=\sqrt{E^2+N^2}

The calculator takes the exact \Delta\gamma_T of each step (4 ft, or 1/60 of a roll cycle if shorter) at its mid-step roll angle. Burning out after a whole number of roll cycles cancels the turn best, and one cycle is usually the minimum; a roll wavelength near 1000 ft is close to optimum over a broad range of pitch wave numbers.

Wind gusts

Above the first correlation distance the gust angle of attack has a nearly constant standard deviation (Hoult, Sounding Rocket Gust Angle of Attack):

\alpha_G=\sigma_G\lambda_P\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_G^*}{2a(\lambda_P^2+\lambda_G^{*2})}}

Its lift, L_A/2ma=\rho SC_{L\alpha}h\alpha_G/2m, drives the same equation as above, giving a planar one-sigma flight path angle

\gamma_G=K\left[\frac{h}{L}-\left(\frac{h}{L}\right)^{g/2a}\right],\qquad K=\frac{\rho SLC_{L\alpha}\sigma_G\lambda_P}{m(2a-g)}\sqrt{\frac{a\lambda_G^*}{2(\lambda_P^2+\lambda_G^{*2})}}

The model needs 2a>g, a thrust above 1.5 times the weight.

Equivalent launcher angle

The two errors come from independent causes, so

\gamma=\sqrt{\gamma_D^2+\gamma_G^2}

evaluated at burnout. Without lift, a launcher error \gamma_0 grows as \gamma=\gamma_0(h/L)^{g/2a}, so the equivalent launcher angle is

\gamma_0=\gamma\left(\frac{h_b}{L}\right)^{-g/2a}

Started from \gamma(L)=\gamma_0, a point-mass trajectory run gives one component of the impact displacement. Both components are normally distributed, unbiased and independent with the same standard deviation, because the misalignment phase is uniform in roll and the gust field has two independent components.

Notes on the method

  • Field tool step. DISPERSION integrates E and N with the step (L_A/mg)(L_A/2ma)\,Z\,\Delta h/h (with a in g), which is L_A/m times the slope of eq. 3 and disagrees with its own \gamma_T column. The error grows near roll resonance: at \lambda_P=0.012 ft⁻¹ the tool gives 0.074 rad where eq. 3 gives 0.014 rad. The memo’s Figure 3 agrees with eq. 3, which the calculator follows.
  • Field tool gust factor. The tool uses \lambda_P^2+\lambda_R^2 where eq. 15 has \lambda_P^2+\lambda_G^{*2}; the gust field is fixed to the earth, so roll does not enter. The difference is a few percent.
  • Acceleration. The tool uses a=T/m; the calculator subtracts gravity, a=T/m-g, as the vertical-flight equations assume. Drag is neglected in both.
