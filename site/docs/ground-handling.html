Ground Handling Loads — theory

The two ground cases that break rockets. Based on C. P. Hoult, Ground Handling Loads, 2014.

Open the calculatorOriginal memo (PDF)

Overview

Ground handling loads live in the shadow of flight and parachute loads, but ground failures happen, and even when repairable they cost schedule. Two cases are known: bending of a slender rocket carried from each end, and tearing the aft riding button off while the rocket is slid onto the launcher rail.

Carried by hand

A rocket of weight W with its centre of gravity at x_{CG}, carried at x_F and x_A, is in equilibrium when

F_F+F_A=W,\qquad F_F(x_{CG}-x_F)=F_A(x_A-x_{CG})\quad\Rightarrow\quad F_F=W\frac{x_A-x_{CG}}{x_A-x_F},\ \ F_A=W\frac{x_{CG}-x_F}{x_A-x_F}

Split the rocket into parts of weight w_i at x_i and make a phantom cut at a possible weak point x_J, such as an interstage joint. Riding-button extraction

To load the rocket, the rail is lowered to horizontal and the aft button is slid into the end of the 80-20 slot. Inserting the forward button, often near mid-body, is hard to see, so the crew raises the nose. A pitch-up of only 1.5–5° brings the nozzle exit plane into contact with the rail; beyond that the rocket pivots on the nozzle, and the aft button is levered out of the rocket like a nail under a claw hammer — with almost no feel.

Before contact, the nose lift and the aft button share the weight:

\frac{F_{F0}}{W}=\frac{x_L-x_{CG}}{x_L-x_F}

After contact the nozzle exit carries a reaction F_A and the button pulls with F_L. Moment and force balances give F_F(x_A-x_F)+F_L(x_L-x_A)=W(x_A-x_{CG}). The angle changes too little to warn the crew, but the force does: if the crew stops at K times the normal lift, a button designed to

F_L=W\,\frac{K(x_L-x_{CG})(x_A-x_F)-(x_A-x_{CG})(x_L-x_F)}{(x_L-x_F)(x_A-x_L)}

will not be extracted. Absent better knowledge use K=2, design both buttons to the same load, and do not give this job to crew members who do not know their own strength.

Verification

The tests reproduce the original field tool’s lift forces and button loads for K=1 to 3 (241.8 lb at K=2 for the 54 lb example, the memo’s “about 243 lb”), check the statics of the carried rocket, and confirm that the joint shear and moment are the same whether computed from the forward or the aft piece.

Notes on the method

  • Joint moment. The memo writes the joint moment as N(F_Fx_F-\sum w_ix_i), which is the moment about the nose tip; it equals the moment at the cut only when the shear there is zero. The calculator takes moments about the cut, and the test checks them against the aft piece.
