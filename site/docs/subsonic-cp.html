Subsonic Centre of Pressure — theory

Barrowman’s method with full NACA Report 1307 interference and a hemispherical nose cap. Based on C. P. Hoult, Barrowman Equations (2010) and Normal Force Acting on a Hemispherical Nose Tip (2010), RS&T memos, and the workbook SUBSONIC BARROWMAN EQUATIONS2.4.xls.

Overview

Static pitch stability depends on where the aerodynamic normal force acts — the centre of pressure (CP) — relative to the centre of gravity. The memo is blunt about folklore alternatives such as balancing a cardboard cut-out or whirling the rocket on a string. For years the amateur community has treated the Barrowman equations — slender-body loading for the body plus Diederich’s approximate lifting-surface theory for the fins — as the standard for subsonic CP estimation.

The workbook keeps Barrowman’s body and fin models and improves three things:

  • a tangent hemispherical nose cap with its own normal-force model when the profile does not start at zero radius;
  • full Prandtl–Glauert compressibility in Diederich’s fin formula;
  • the full slender-body wing–body and body–wing interference of NACA Report 1307.

Everything is linear in angle of attack: each part contributes a normal-force coefficient slope C_{N\alpha} (per radian, on S_{ref}=\pi d^2/4) acting at its own station.

Body of revolution — slender-body theory

Munk’s slender-body theory gives the normal force per unit length as proportional to the rate of change of cross-section area, dC_N/dx=(2/S_{ref})\,dA/dx\cdot\alpha. For a segment between profile points i-1 and i:

C_{N\alpha,i}=\frac{2\,(A_i-A_{i-1})}{S_{ref}},\qquad A_i=\pi r_i^2 .

Its centre of pressure follows from the moment of dA/dx; with the frustum volume V_i,

V_i=\frac{A_i(x_i-x_{i-1})}{3}\left(\frac{A_{i-1}}{A_i}+\frac{r_{i-1}}{r_i}+1\right),\qquad x_{cp,i}=\frac{x_iA_i-x_{i-1}A_{i-1}-V_i}{A_i-A_{i-1}} .

Cylinders (A_i=A_{i-1}) carry no normal force; a boat-tail (decreasing area) carries negative normal force. The body totals are

C_{N\alpha,body}=\sum_i C_{N\alpha,i},\qquad M_{body}=\sum_i C_{N\alpha,i}\,x_{cp,i}.

For a pointed nose this sums to the classic slender-body result C_{N\alpha}=2A_{base}/S_{ref}=2.

Hemispherical nose cap

If the first profile radius r_0 is not zero, a hemisphere is fitted tangent to the first segment. Its tangency angle and radius are

\theta_T=\frac\pi2-\tan^{-1}\frac{r_1-r_0}{x_1-x_0},\qquad R_h=\frac{r_0}{\sin\theta_T}.

The memo starts from the incompressible pressure distribution on a sphere, C_p=1-\tfrac94\sin^2\theta, measures \theta from the displaced stagnation point (\theta'=\theta-\alpha\cos\varphi), keeps first-order terms in \alpha and integrates over the cap up to \theta_T:

C_{N\alpha,cap}=\frac{9\pi}{8}\,\frac{R_h^2\sin^4\theta_T}{S_{ref}}.

A full hemisphere on a body of the same radius gives C_{N\alpha}=9/8, well below the slender-body value of 2. The cap CP behind its own tip is

\frac{x_{cp}}{R_h}=\frac{\sin^4\theta_T-4\left[\tfrac15\cos\theta_T\sin^4\theta_T-\tfrac1{15}\cos\theta_T(\sin^2\theta_T+2)+\tfrac2{15}\right]}{\sin^4\theta_T},

and, remarkably, the same result follows from modified Newtonian flow — the cap CP is independent of Mach number. In body stations (station 0 at the tangency point) the workbook places it at R_h(\cos\theta_T-1)+x_{cp}. A sphere’s critical Mach number is about 0.41, so below that a hemispherical tip has no wave-drag penalty.

If the tip is a flat face instead, the cap’s normal force is zero and the workbook adds a small flat-face pitching-moment term (see Known differences).

Isolated fins — Diederich with compressibility

Opposite panels form a wing. For one panel with root chord c_R, tip chord c_T, exposed semispan b, root and tip leading-edge stations x_R, x_T (and a boat-tail half-angle \beta under the root, usually zero):

S=\frac{c_R^2\tan\beta}{2}+\frac{(c_R+c_T)(b-c_R\tan\beta)}{2},\qquad l=b-\tfrac34c_R\tan\beta,\qquad A\!R=\frac{2l^2}{S},
\Lambda_c=\tan^{-1}\frac{x_T+c_T/4-x_R-c_R/4}{b}\quad\text{(quarter-chord sweep)}.

With the Prandtl–Glauert factor \beta_\Lambda=\sqrt{1-M^2\cos^2\Lambda_c}, Diederich’s compressible plan-form parameter and lift slope (on the area of the panel pair) are

F=\frac{A\!R\,\beta_\Lambda}{\cos\Lambda_c},\qquad C_{N\alpha,W}=\frac{2\pi F\cos\Lambda_c}{\beta_\Lambda\left(F\sqrt{1+4/F^2}+2\right)} .

For N panels on the body reference area,

C_{N\alpha,F}=\frac N2\,\frac{S}{S_{ref}}\,C_{N\alpha,W}.

The fin CP is a quarter of the mean aerodynamic chord behind its leading edge:

\bar c=\tfrac23\left(c_R+c_T-\frac{c_Rc_T}{c_R+c_T}\right),\qquad x_F=x_R+\frac{\bar c}4+\frac{(c_R+2c_T)(x_T-x_R)}{3(c_R+c_T)} .

Fin–body interference (NACA Report 1307)

The body increases the lift on the fins, and the fins induce lift on the body. With r the body radius at the quarter root chord, s=r+b the fin tip radius and \tau=r/s, the slender-body factors are

K_{W(B)}=\frac{2}{\pi(1-\tau)^2}\left[(1+\tau^4)\left(\tfrac12\tan^{-1}\tfrac12\big(\tfrac1\tau-\tau\big)+\tfrac\pi4\right)-\tau^2\left(\tfrac1\tau-\tau+2\tan^{-1}\tau\right)\right],
K_{B(W)}=\frac{(1-\tau^2)^2-\tfrac2\pi\left[(1+\tau^4)\left(\tfrac12\tan^{-1}\tfrac12\big(\tfrac1\tau-\tau\big)+\tfrac\pi4\right)-\tau^2\left(\tfrac1\tau-\tau+2\tan^{-1}\tau\right)\right]}{(1-\tau)^2},

which satisfy K_{W(B)}+K_{B(W)}=(1+\tau)^2, tending to 1 and 0 with no body and to 2 and 2 for vanishing fins. The tail-assembly normal force and moment are

C_{N\alpha,tail}=C_{N\alpha,F}\left(K_{W(B)}+K_{B(W)}\right),\qquad M_{tail}=x_F\,C_{N\alpha,F}K_{W(B)}+\bar x_{B(W)}\,C_{N\alpha,F}K_{B(W)},

where the body carry-over lift acts at (NACA 1307 eq. D6, as coded in the workbook)

\bar x_{B(W)}=x_R+\frac{c_R}4+(s-r)\tan\Lambda_c\left[\frac{r}{r-s}+\frac{\sqrt{s(s-2r)}\cosh^{-1}\frac{s-r}{r}-s+r+\frac{\pi r}{2}}{\frac{(s-r)\,r}{\sqrt{s(s-2r)}}\cosh^{-1}\frac{s-r}{r}+\frac{(s-r)^2}{r}-\frac{\pi(s-r)}{2}}\right].

For short fins (s<2r) the square root and the inverse hyperbolic cosine are both imaginary but their products are real: \sqrt{s(s-2r)}\cosh^{-1}y\to-\sqrt{s(2r-s)}\cos^{-1}y and \cosh^{-1}y/\sqrt{s(s-2r)}\to\cos^{-1}y/\sqrt{s(2r-s)}. The calculator uses this analytic continuation, which is continuous at s=2r; the workbook formula is undefined there.

The workbook also reports the single-panel normal force per radian of fin deflection, C_{N\delta}=(k_{W(B)}+k_{B(W)})\,C_{N\alpha,W}\,S/S_{ref}, with k_{W(B)} from NACA 1307 eq. (19) and k_{B(W)}=K_{W(B)}-k_{W(B)}, eq. (33).

Vehicle CP and static margin

C_{N\alpha}=C_{N\alpha,body}+C_{N\alpha,tail},\qquad X_{CP}=\frac{C_{m\alpha,flat}+M_{body}+M_{tail}}{C_{N\alpha}},

measured aft of the nose tip (station 0). With the CG at X_{CG}, the static margin in calibres is

SM=\frac{X_{CP}-X_{CG}}{d}.

Positive means the CP is behind the CG and the rocket is statically stable. The static margin and its 1–2 calibre guideline are additions by this site (common amateur practice, not from the workbook).

Assumptions and limits

  • Subsonic flow below the critical Mach number; small angles of attack; linear aerodynamics.
  • One fin set. The two-stage vortex-interference model of the workbook (and the 1307 Supplement memo) is not ported — see below.
  • Slender-body theory for the body: no viscous cross-flow lift, which grows at larger angles of attack.
  • Fins are flat, trapezoidal and planar, mounted on the body radius at their quarter root chord.

Worked example (Gold Rush III, ignition)

Mach 0.05; 4.12 in diameter, 93.16 in long, blunt flat tip of 0.05 in radius; six fins with c_R=10, c_T=3, b=4 in, root LE at 83.16 in and tip LE at 90.16 in. Profile exactly as the workbook evaluates it (the 17.5 in point is skipped):

  1. Body: C_{N\alpha,body}=1.9994, moment 18.883 in.
  2. Fins: A\!R=1.2308, \Lambda_c=52.7°, F=2.0299, C_{N\alpha,W}=1.5946, C_{N\alpha,F}=9.3295; x_F=87.814 in.
  3. Interference with \tau=2.06/6.06: K_{W(B)}=1.2907, K_{B(W)}=0.5047; carry-over CP 87.134 in.
  4. Tail: C_{N\alpha,tail}=16.750 at 87.623 in.
  5. Vehicle: C_{N\alpha}=18.750 /rad, X_{CP}=79.286 in from the tip.

Known differences from the workbook

  • Skipped profile row. Row 13 of the workbook (station 17.5 in, radius 2.028 in) has no formulas, so the workbook treats 15 → 20 in as a single frustum. The calculator uses every point you enter; the “exactly as the workbook” example omits that point to reproduce the published result.
  • Two-stage configuration not ported. The second-stage fin row and the fin–fin vortex-interference cells (AF26–AQ26) evaluate to #VALUE! for the published inputs (a logarithm of a negative number), and the vehicle totals use them only when “Number of Fin Sets” is 2. Only the single-fin-set path is implemented.
  • Short fins. For s<2r the workbook’s carry-over CP formula fails; the calculator uses its analytic continuation (see Interference).
  • Body radius at the fins and boat-tail angle. The workbook takes the radius at the quarter root chord as a typed input (Q26) and the boat-tail angle from fixed rows 14–15 (D6). The calculator interpolates the radius from the profile and asks for the boat-tail angle under the fins directly.
  • Station-0 area. The workbook sets the cross-section at station 0 to half the tip disc, K6 = πr₀²/2, so the first segment’s normal force is 2(A_1-A_0/2)/S_{ref}. Neither memo explains this; it is reproduced as published.
  • Flat-face moment term. For a flat tip the workbook adds P6 = −0.0054·0.85·(1+(M²+M⁴)/4)·(2r₀/d) to the moment sum (in inches); it is dimensionally inconsistent but negligible (−1×10⁻⁴ in for Gold Rush III). Reproduced as published.
  • Additions. CG input and static margin; the Mach sweeps and build-up plot.
