Fin Cant Angle & Roll Rate — theory

Strip theory for setting fin angles. Based on C. P. Hoult, Strip Theory for Setting Fin Angles (rev. 3), RS&T memo, 22 May 2014, and the workbook CANT ANGLE3.xls.

Overview

Fin warpage makes every fin-stabilised rocket roll at random and adds random pitch/yaw moments. Setting the fins deliberately serves two purposes:

  • Dispersion. A well-chosen roll-rate profile averages out the impact-point errors caused by body-fixed misalignments, especially thrust misalignment.
  • Loads. Fin misalignments add angle of attack, and with it structural load on the fins and the whole airframe.

The process is: measure the as-built cant angles, null each panel’s misalignment normal force, then set all panels to one common cant angle that produces the planned roll rate. This page derives the roll derivatives and that common angle.

Strip theory

Each fin panel is divided into streamwise strips of span dy, each with the same normal-force slope c_{N\alpha}. There is no fin–body interference and no spanwise variation of lift slope from tip losses — the simplest model that accounts for planform geometry. Thin-airfoil theory makes c_{N\alpha} depend only on Mach number; using a panel-averaged value captures root and tip losses approximately.

A strip at radius y from the body centre line, on a fin canted by \delta and rolling at rate p, sees the local angle of attack \delta-py/U. Its normal force and rolling moment are

dN_T=q\,c\,c_{N\alpha}\left(\delta-\frac{py}{U}\right)dy,\qquad dL=y\,dN_T=q\,y\,c\,c_{N\alpha}\left(\delta-\frac{py}{U}\right)dy .

For a straight-tapered fin running from the body radius R to the tip radius b, the chord is linear:

c=A-Cy,\qquad A=\frac{c_Rb-c_TR}{b-R},\qquad C=\frac{c_R-c_T}{b-R}.

Integrating over the panel gives the rolling moment of one fin,

L=q\,c_{N\alpha}(b-R)\,\delta\,L_\delta+q\,c_{N\alpha}(b-R)\frac{p}{U}\,M,

where the two planform integrals are

L_\delta=\frac{A(b+R)}{2}-\frac{C(b^2+bR+R^2)}{3},\qquad M=-\frac{A(b^2+bR+R^2)}{3}+\frac{C(b^3+b^2R+bR^2+R^3)}{4}.

The spanwise location of the normal force due to cant is

Y_{CP}=\frac{3A(b+R)-2C(b^2+bR+R^2)}{6A-3C(b+R)}.

Roll stability derivatives

With reference area S=\pi R^2 and reference length d=2R, the rolling-moment coefficient is C_L=L/(qSd)=L/(2\pi R^3q). The roll-driving derivative of a single fin is

C_{L\delta}=\frac{\partial C_L}{\partial\delta}=\frac{c_{N\alpha}(b-R)}{2\pi R^3}\,L_\delta,

and the roll-damping derivative of all N fins, per unit pR/U, is

C_{Lp}=\frac{\partial C_L}{\partial(pR/U)}=\frac{c_{N\alpha}(b-R)N}{2\pi R^4}\,M .

M is negative, so C_{Lp} is negative: rolling always generates an opposing moment.

Fin adjustment and the cant angle for a roll rate

Many adjustments are possible, but the preferred one sets every panel to the same cant after nulling its normal force. For three or more panels this cancels the combined pitch and yaw moments and gives all panels the same cant-induced loads. In the field the as-built cant is measured with a laser level aligned with the body axis: the spot’s height on a screen at the nose tip shows the cant, which is then adjusted to the desired value.

In steady roll the driving and damping moments balance, L=0, so the common cant for a roll rate p at speed U is

\delta=-\frac{p}{U}\,\frac{M}{L_\delta}=-\frac{pR\,C_{Lp}}{N\,U\,C_{L\delta}}.

This is what the workbook’s “fixed burnout roll rate” block computes. The memo’s expression also includes the lag caused by roll inertia during the boost:

\delta=-\frac{pR\,C_{Lp}}{N\,U\,C_{L\delta}}\,(1-J),\qquad J=\frac{I_{xx}}{2\pi\rho\,(h-L)\,R^4C_{Lp}},

where h is the burnout altitude and L the launcher length. The memo considers this good enough for small rockets up to about 8 inches in diameter; for larger ones it suggests fitting the measured cant data, the simplest fit being a helix \delta(y)=(-\delta_1y_2+\delta_2y_1)/(y_1-y_2)+y(\delta_1-\delta_2)/(y_1-y_2) through two spanwise measurements.

Choosing the roll rate

For multistage rockets, set the last stage first and work down. For a single stage, the mean cant \bar\delta_F for a given roll wavenumber \lambda_R is

\lambda_R=-\frac{C_{L\delta}N\bar\delta_F}{RC_{Lp}}\left[\frac{2\pi\rho(h-L)R^4C_{Lp}}{2\pi\rho(h-L)R^4C_{Lp}-I_{xx}}\right].

Whole roll cycles. To minimise dispersion from body-fixed perturbations, the booster should complete a whole number M of roll cycles between launcher exit and burnout:

\Delta\phi_{BO}=2\pi M=-\frac{C_{L\delta}N\bar\delta_F}{RC_{Lp}}\left[h-L+k\ln\!\left(\frac{h-L-k}{-k}\right)\right],\qquad k=\frac{I_{xx}}{2\pi\rho R^4C_{Lp}},

which is solved for \bar\delta_F. M should be at least 2 or 3; more is better. This choice matters mainly for the booster.

Fraction of the pitch wavenumber. Alternatively, for any stage, choose \lambda_R as a fraction of the pitch wavenumber \lambda_P. Body-fixed perturbations produce an angle-of-attack response proportional to \lambda_P^2/(\lambda_P^2-\lambda_R^2), so roll erodes static stability; as long as \lambda_R stays below 30–40 % of \lambda_P the damage is small. A higher roll rate still reduces dispersion. The memo’s example sets the burnout roll rate to 0.3 × the burnout pitch natural frequency, about 5 roll cycles to burnout.

Assumptions and limits

  • Linear strip theory: no fin–body interference, constant c_{N\alpha} along the span, small angles.
  • Straight-tapered fins; the root is at the body surface.
  • In practice, errors in measuring misalignment and cant tend to dominate everything else, so one usually only aims for a desired roll rate.
  • The calculator warns above 3° of cant; that threshold is this site’s guidance.

Worked example (Gold Rush III)

R=3.125 in, b=5.5 in, c_R=10 in, c_T=2 in, 4 fins, c_{N\alpha}=3.5/rad:

  1. A=20.526 in, C=3.368, L_\delta=24.292 in², M=-100.75 in³, Y_{CP}=4.049 in.
  2. C_{L\delta}=3.5(2.375)(24.292)/(2\pi\,3.125^3)=1.0531 /rad (memo); C_{Lp}=-5.5906.
  3. For 1.24 Hz at 1037.2 ft/s: p/U=6.26\times10^{-4} /in and \delta=0.1488° (steady state). With I_{xx}=0.22 slug·ft², \rho=0.0023, h=2000 ft and L=18 ft, J=-0.299 and the memo’s cant is 0.193°.
  4. For 4 roll cycles to burnout: k=-592.1 ft, bracket 1111.9 ft, \delta=0.448° (memo C_{L\delta}) or 0.230° (workbook C_{L\delta}).

Known differences from the workbook

  • Roll-driving derivative, cell Q5 (fidelity switch). The workbook has =O5*(D5-B5)*L5/(2+PI()*C5^3), where B5 holds π: that is c_{N\alpha}(b-\pi)L_\delta/(2+\pi R^3) instead of the memo’s c_{N\alpha}(b-R)L_\delta/(2\pi R^3). For Gold Rush III it gives 2.0487 instead of 1.0531, so the roll-cycle cant comes out 0.230° instead of 0.448°. Strip integration confirms the memo’s value. The calculator uses the memo by default; “Match original spreadsheet” reproduces the workbook. The steady-state roll-rate result (G9/H9) does not use C_{L\delta} and is the same in both modes.
  • Roll-inertia lag. The workbook’s roll-rate block omits the memo’s (1-J) factor; the calculator shows both.
  • File name. The memo refers to “CANT ANGLE2.xls”; the published workbook is CANT ANGLE3.xls.
